%--------------------RESEARCH INTERESTS-----------------------------------
\section*{Research Focus}
My research focuses on \textbf{AI alignment through reinforcement learning (RL) for fair, safe, and socially aligned decision making}. I develop RL methods that enable autonomous decision-making systems to account for competing objectives, stakeholder preferences, and safety requirements. My work spans multi-objective and preference-based RL, multi-agent systems, and alignment of learned decision-making systems, with applications to autonomous and real-world systems.

%--------------------RESEARCH SUMMARY-----------------------------------
% \section*{Research Program}
% My research asks how reinforcement learning systems should aggregate competing objectives when the standard answer---a weighted linear sum---is inadequate. Linear scalarization is the default in multi-objective RL, in multi-agent credit assignment, and in the reward models and rubric verifiers used to align large language models. It is also the mechanism by which a system can appear well-behaved on average while failing badly on a particular objective, agent, or evaluation criterion.

% I formulate these problems instead as optimization of nonlinear social welfare functions---Generalized Gini, Nash, and maximin---over per-objective returns, which makes such failures visible and penalized rather than averaged away. This framing began with fairness in multi-objective RL (ICML 2020), extended to decentralized cooperative multi-agent settings (ICML 2021), to preference-based RL where the welfare function is learned from human comparisons (\textit{Machine Learning Journal}), and most recently to LLM alignment, where I replace linear rubric aggregation with nonlinear welfare functions and move the scalarization inside the expectation (COLM 2026 workshop).

% A second, complementary line concerns \emph{inference-time} alignment: adapting an already-trained policy to new fairness or safety criteria at deployment without retraining (RLC 2026, and an extension to safety constraints currently under review). A third applies these methods to autonomous systems under real resource constraints, supported by the Office of Naval Research and the Army Research Laboratory.
